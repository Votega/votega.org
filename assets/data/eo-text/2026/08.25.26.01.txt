THE STATE OF GEORGIA
                                    EXECUTIVE ORDER


BY THE GOVERNOR:


                            FLAGS ON ALL STATE BUILDINGS AND GROUNDS
                                ORDERED LOWERED TO HALF STAFF


   WHEREAS: Dolly Rebecca Parton Dean was an exceptional talent, singer, songwriter, actress,
            philanthropist, businesswoman, and American treasure, as well as a woman of
            joyful and enduring faith; and

   WHEREAS: Born in a one-room cabin in Tennessee in the Smoky Mountains, Dolly was the
            fourth of twelve children and grew up in a family she called “dirt poor,” but one
            rich in faith and joy – an upbringing she memorialized in songs like “My Tennessee
            Mountain Home,” and “Coat of Many Colors;” and

   WHEREAS: Dolly moved to Nashville the day after graduating high school to begin what would
            ultimately be an historic country music career, meeting the man who would
            become her husband of nearly 59 years, Carl Thomas Dean, the very same day. Two
            years later in 1966, they married in Ringgold, Georgia, in part because the town’s
            name reminded her of "rings of gold," which she took as a good sign for her
            marriage; and

   WHEREAS: Spanning over seven decades, Dolly’s extraordinary career earned her 25 No. 1
            country singles; 11 Grammy Awards; induction into both the Country Music Hall
            of Fame and the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame; many arts and humanities honors;
            starring roles in movies like “9 to 5,” “Steel Magnolias,” Joyful Noise,” and others;
            numerous television appearances; and fame around the world; and

   WHEREAS: Of all her many achievements and honors, perhaps the most long-lasting and
            meaningful is Dolly Parton's abiding place in the hearts and memories of so many
            who appreciated her genuine kindness, her larger-than-life talents, and her
            sincerity when she sang those timeless lyrics: “I will always love you;” and

   WHEREAS: It is fitting and proper to honor Dolly Parton’s memory by lowering the flags on all
            State buildings and grounds to half-staff.
           NOW, THEREFORE, IN HONOR AND AS A MARK OF RESPECT FOR THE MEMORY OF
           DOLLY PARTON, PURSUANT TO THE AUTHORITY VESTED IN ME AS GOVERNOR OF
           THE STATE OF GEORGIA, IT IS HEREBY

ORDERED:   That the flag of the United States and the flag of the State of Georgia be flown at
           half-staff on all state buildings and grounds for a one-week period beginning at
           sunrise on Wednesday, August 26, 2026.

           This 25th day of August 2026.
