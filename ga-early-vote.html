Turnout and education, county by county

Each dot is a county: the share of its adults 25 and over with a bachelor’s degree, against the share who have already voted. Switch the measure to see the same counties against their citizen adults or against their registered voters — the two disagree most where students and soldiers live. Diamonds voted Republican and circles Democratic in {{ lean_last }}. Tap or hover a dot to read it.

This compares counties; it says nothing about any one voter. Education stands in for many things at once (age, income, how urban a county is). A county with a large university or military post sits low against citizen adults, because students and service members are counted among its adults while many of them are registered to vote somewhere else; switching the measure to active registered voters drops that effect, since it counts only people registered in the county. Education and adult-population counts are estimates from the Census Bureau’s American Community Survey ({{ ev.metadata.countyContext.vintage }} 5-year, via Georgia Civic Data, CC BY 4.0), and small counties carry wide margins of error. “Citizen adults” is residents 18 and over minus non-citizen residents of every age, so it runs slightly low. Turnout is ballots cast divided by active registered voters. "Early share of turnout" is the share of a past election's votes cast in advance or by mail, from the Secretary of State's results export; it reflects each year's rules, early voting sites and type of election, so 2022 and 2024 are not directly comparable. The ’22 D and ’24 R tags beside a county name show which party's candidate won more than half of that county's votes for Governor in 2022 and President in 2024 (third parties count in the total); a dash would mean no one cleared half. Hover or tap a tag for the percentage. "Also voted early" is the share of this year's early and absentee voters who also cast an accepted early or absentee ballot in that election (any date); it can't tell a first-time voter from someone who voted on election day. For 2022 it comes from the Secretary of State's voter history file, because the 2022 absentee file is missing most Fulton and Gwinnett ballots; the history file matches the official early and mail totals but has no return dates, which is why the 2022 daily comparison still stops at 17 days out. Past years count accepted ballots returned by the same number of days before each election day ({% for y in ev.metadata.comparisons %}{{ y[0] }}: {{ y[1].cutoff | date: "%B %-d" }}{% unless forloop.last %}; {% endunless %}{% endfor %}).
