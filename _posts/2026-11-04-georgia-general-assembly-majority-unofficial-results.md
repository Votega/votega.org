## What the numbers show

| Chamber | Current roster | Projected | Majority line |
|---|---|---|---|
| House | 99 R, 81 D (one Democratic seat suspended) | **92 D, 88 R** | 91 |
| Senate | 33 R, 23 D | **27 D, 29 R** | 29 |

- **House:** 13 seats change from Republican to Democratic and 2 from Democratic to Republican, a net gain of 11 for Democrats. That would put Democrats 1 seat above the 91-seat majority line — so a single district moving in the canvass would take the chamber to a 90&ndash;90 tie.
- **Senate:** 4 seats change from Republican to Democratic. Democrats would be 2 seats short of the 29-seat majority line, and Republicans would sit exactly on it.
- **Narrow margins:** nine seats are decided by under 2 points. The closest is House district 103, where 28 votes separate the candidates out of 23,984 cast, followed by House districts 29 (135 votes) and 22 (274). Senate district 37 is the only Senate seat inside 2 points, at 1,409 votes. House district 154 was in this group on November 4 and has already changed hands once; these are the seats most likely to move again in the canvass or a recount.
- **Unopposed seats:** seats marked in amber had no major-party opponent, so they were never contested between the two parties.

A bill needs a majority of all the members the chamber is entitled to, whether or not every seat can vote (Ga. Const. Art. III, Sec. V, Para. V), so the majority line is 91 in the House and 29 in the Senate regardless of vacancies.

## Method

Seats are filled by the leading candidate in each district from the Secretary of State's unofficial results, which also feed the [2026 results page]({{ '/ga-general-2026-results/' | relative_url }}). "Flipped" compares the projected party with the party of the member on the current [Majority Tracker]({{ '/ga-majority-tracker.html' | relative_url }}) roster. Names shown are the projected winners. This post does not account for recounts, runoffs or later changes to the results.
