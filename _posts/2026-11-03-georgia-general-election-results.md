Georgia's **November 3, 2026** general election results are on one page: the [2026 General Election Results page]({{ '/ga-general-2026-results/' | relative_url }}). It covers every race on the statewide ballot — U.S. Senate, U.S. House, Governor and the rest of the statewide executive offices, the Public Service Commission, the General Assembly, the courts, District Attorney races, and the statewide ballot questions.

Rather than describe numbers that change, this post explains how to read the page and where the figures come from. The page itself is the live record.

## How the page works

- **Every race is listed before it reports.** A race reads "Awaiting Results" until votes are counted for it, so a race missing from the page is a gap on our side, not a race with no returns.
- **Counts replace the placeholder on their own** as results are posted, and keep updating.
- **A "last updated" time** appears on the page while results are unofficial. The counts are built into the page rather than streamed, so check that time to see how current the numbers you are reading are.
- **Search and filters** narrow more than 450 contests to the ones you care about — by office, by candidate name, or by party.
- **Tabs** split the ballot into statewide, U.S. House, State Senate, State House, courts, and ballot questions.
- **Candidate names link** to their profiles where we have one, with their fundraising, their record, and the rest of what they ran on.

## Unofficial, then certified

Election-night and next-day numbers are **unofficial**. Counties keep counting after polls close: provisional ballots, eligible military and overseas ballots, and cured absentee ballots are still being resolved. Counties then certify, and the Secretary of State certifies the statewide result after them. Margins move in that window, and close races can change.

The page says which stage it is in. Once the state certifies, it switches to **certified** results and stops changing — the numbers then are the official record.

A statewide race in which no candidate wins a majority goes to a **December 1 runoff**, which gets its own results page linked from the same place.

## Where the numbers come from

Vote counts are the Georgia Secretary of State's own reported totals. We do not call races, project winners, or adjust counts; a candidate shown as leading is leading in the count reported so far, which is not the same as having won.
