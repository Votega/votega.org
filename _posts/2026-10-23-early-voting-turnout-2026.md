> **See the live numbers:** The [Early Voting Turnout page]({{ '/ga-early-vote/' | relative_url }}) has every county, the daily pace, and the mail ballot pipeline. It is refreshed each time the Secretary of State posts a new file.

Early voting for the **November 3, 2026** general election began on October 13. As of **October 29**, Georgians had cast **2,513,642** early in-person and accepted mail ballots, about 33.4% of the state's active voters.

A raw count means little without a comparison, so the page lines 2026 up against past elections at the same number of days before election day.

## What the numbers show so far

- **Against 2022, the last midterm:** at 17 days out, 2026 is about 1.7% ahead in ballots, and level when measured against the estimated number of citizen adults (10.8% in both years). Early in-person voting is up about 8%, while accepted mail ballots are down by more than half.
- **Against 2024:** at 5 days out, 2026 is roughly 32% behind. 2024 was a presidential year, which draws far more voters, so a midterm is expected to trail it.

Early voting runs through **Friday, October 30**, and mail ballots keep arriving until the polls close, so these figures will keep moving.

## What else is on the page

- **A county table** with ballots cast, turnout as a share of active voters and of estimated citizen adults, and mail share. Each county carries small tags showing which party carried it for Governor in 2022 and President in 2024, and you can filter by party.
- **The daily pace** of early voting, lined up by days before election day, for 2026, 2022, and 2024.
- **The mail ballot pipeline** by county: how many are accepted, still outstanding, rejected, or cancelled.
- **Turnout and education**, a scatter plot of all 159 counties. Counties with more college graduates have voted at higher rates so far. That is a pattern across counties, not something that says anything about an individual voter.

## What to keep in mind

{: .box-note}
**The 2022 comparison stops at 17 days out.** The Secretary of State's 2022 absentee file is missing most ballots from Fulton and Gwinnett counties after October 22, 2022. We compare 2022 only up to the last complete day instead of guessing at the rest.

**Citizen adults is an estimate.** It is adults 18 and over minus non-citizen residents, from Census Bureau estimates compiled by [Georgia Civic Data](https://georgiacivicdata.org). It runs slightly low, so treat it as a rough yardstick. Active voters is the more precise rate.

Where the numbers come from, and how each is calculated, is documented on the [About the Data]({{ '/about-the-data' | relative_url }}#early-voting-turnout) page.
