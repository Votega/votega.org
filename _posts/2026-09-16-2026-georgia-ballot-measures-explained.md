At the bottom of your **November 3, 2026** ballot, past the candidates, you'll find a short list of statewide measures written in dense legal language. They're easy to skip but a "yes" or "no" here changes Georgia's constitution or its tax code directly, with no candidate in between. This is where your vote is most literal.

Here's each measure in plain English: what it does, and what your vote means.

{: .box-note}
**How Georgia amendments work.** A constitutional amendment must first pass the General Assembly by a two-thirds vote in both chambers, then win a simple majority of voters statewide. That's why these appear as a plain **YES / NO** question. A referendum works the same way at the ballot but changes state law rather than the constitution.

## The three certified constitutional amendments

These three have cleared every step and **will** appear on the ballot.

### 1. Next Generation 9-1-1 Fund

*Enabling legislation: [HR 1243](https://www.legis.ga.gov/legislation/72891){:target="_blank"}*

**What it does:** Lets the state legislature create a dedicated "Georgia Next Generation 9-1-1 Fund" and set aside revenue that can only be spent on modernizing the state's 911 emergency systems.

- **A YES vote** allows the creation of this restricted-use fund, meaning money raised for 911 upgrades must be spent on 911 upgrades and not diverted elsewhere.
- **A NO vote** leaves 911 funding without that constitutional protection.

### 2. Conservation-Use Acreage Limit for Farms and Timberland

*Enabling legislation: [HR 32](https://www.legis.ga.gov/legislation/69433){:target="_blank"}*

**What it does:** Georgia taxes "bona fide conservation use" agricultural and timber land at 40% of its value a break meant to keep working land in production. This measure raises the maximum acreage that can qualify from **2,000 acres to 4,000 acres**.

- **A YES vote** doubles the acreage cap, extending the lower-tax conservation classification to larger farm and timber operations.
- **A NO vote** keeps the current 2,000-acre limit.

### 3. Nonpartisan Elections for Probate Judges

*Enabling legislation: [HR 251](https://www.legis.ga.gov/legislation/70279){:target="_blank"}*

**What it does:** Requires that Georgia's probate judges who handle wills, estates, some marriage and firearms licenses, and in many counties election administration — be elected **without party labels** on the ballot.

- **A YES vote** removes party affiliation (D/R) from probate judge races statewide.
- **A NO vote** keeps probate judges running under party labels where they currently do.

## One measure still pending

### Homestead Tax Exemption for Families of Disabled Veterans

*Related legislation: [HB 52](https://www.legis.ga.gov/legislation/69349){:target="_blank"} — statewide referendum*

**What it would do:** Extend Georgia's homestead property-tax exemption to the **surviving spouses and minor children of disabled veterans**.

{: .box-note}
As of our last data update, this measure was **potential, not yet certified**. It passed the General Assembly but still required final steps to be referred to the ballot. Check the [live tracker]({{ '/ga-ballot-measures.html' | relative_url }}) for its current status before you vote.

- **A YES vote** (if it appears) would grant the exemption to those surviving family members.
- **A NO vote** would leave the exemption unchanged.

## Why these are worth your time

Amendments and referendums pass or fail on turnout that's often a fraction of the top-of-ticket vote; which means each ballot counts for more here. They also stick around a constitutional amendment isn't undone at the next election the way a policy fight might be.

A few minutes of prep pays off:

1. Read each measure above and decide your **YES / NO** before Election Day.
2. Preview your full ballot  with the [Sample Ballot tool]({{ '/sample-ballot' | relative_url }}).
3. Confirm your registration and polling place at [mvp.sos.ga.gov](https://mvp.sos.ga.gov){:target="_blank"} (registration deadline: **October 5, 2026**).
