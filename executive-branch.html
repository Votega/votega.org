Federal Executive Branch

The Executive Branch of the federal government is headed by the President and Vice President, who are elected together every four years. The President's Cabinet consists of the Vice President and the heads of executive departments, each confirmed by the U.S. Senate.

The President's Cabinet

The Cabinet includes the Vice President and the heads of 15 executive departments. All department secretaries are nominated by the President and confirmed by the U.S. Senate.
